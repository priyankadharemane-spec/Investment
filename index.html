Questions worth asking

Clear answers before you decide.

These answers summarise the supplied materials and identify where final documentation or independent advice is necessary.

General

What is Vanatvam’s investment approach?+

Vanatvam develops natural sustainable communities across Karnataka, combining land, biodiversity-led landscape planning and nature-based experiences. The supplied Growth Fund material describes a repeatable cycle: acquire land, restore the landscape, package the offering, monetise it and reinvest. The two opportunities presented here are different in structure: a convertible debenture and a project-specific land investment. Review the relevant brochure and agreements before deciding.

How is Vanatvam different from a conventional land seller?+

Vanatvam’s materials describe land development as more than a transaction. Projects bring together native planting, habitat enhancement, common spaces and community experiences, with nature treated as part of the place-making process. The company presentation reports 100+ acres across Karnataka and 200+ tree varieties. These are company-provided figures; investors should verify current project details and the exact scope of each offering independently.

Can I compare both options before choosing?+

Yes. Start by comparing what you are investing in, how the investment is documented, the expected holding period, how value or payments may arise, and how you may exit. A debenture is a company-issued instrument governed by its subscription terms; the Harale land opportunity is project-specific and uses a proposed land-linked ownership and revenue-sharing structure. They are not interchangeable, and neither should be selected on a headline figure alone.

Debenture Investment

What is a convertible debenture in simple terms?+

A convertible debenture is a documented investment instrument issued by a company that may include an option to convert into equity under specified conditions. Vanatvam’s Growth Fund material describes a minimum subscription of ₹10 lakh, additional amounts in multiples of ₹5 lakh, and a conversion option up to Year 5. The final rights, payment terms and conversion mechanics must be checked in the executed offer and subscription documents.

What minimum amount and tenure are shown for the Growth Fund?+

The supplied Growth Fund presentation states a minimum investment of ₹10 lakh, with additional investments in multiples of ₹5 lakh. It describes a 15–18% per annum return band and a 2–3 year tenure, with repayment targeted by Year 3 and an extension or conversion option up to Year 5. These are stated offer terms and illustrations, not a promise that any return or repayment will occur.

Are debenture returns guaranteed, and how are payouts handled?+

No return should be treated as guaranteed. The Growth Fund material describes annual payout or compounding at maturity and a principal-plus-return repayment target by Year 3, subject to the debenture terms. Actual payment depends on the executed documents and the issuer’s ability to meet its obligations. Before subscribing, review default provisions, extensions, conversion rights, tax treatment and all relevant risks with qualified advisers.

Land Investment

What is the Harale riverfront land investment?+

The project briefing describes an 11.5-acre first phase at Harale, near Kollegala in Karnataka, adjoining the Kaveri River. The concept is a biodiversity-led riverfront community, with native planting, low-impact planning and nature-based experiences. The briefing notes a 24–30 month indicative project horizon. Site facts, title, permissions, final design, costs and timing must be independently verified and may change.

How does the proposed land-investment structure work?+

The Harale briefing describes investor capital being deployed toward land acquisition at an indicated basis of ₹45 lakh per acre, plus estimated transaction costs, with Vanatvam leading planning, development and marketing under agreements. It illustrates a 45% investor/landowner share of gross project revenue in example scenarios. The final ownership, cost allocation, revenue calculation, obligations and exit terms must be defined in signed documents; illustrations are not guarantees.

What are plantation investment returns in a land-linked project?+

Land-linked outcomes can depend on acquisition cost, approvals, development expenditure, buyer demand, selling price, taxes and the time taken to complete sales. The Harale briefing includes example scenarios using different selling prices and a proposed 45% investor/landowner revenue share, but explicitly says outcomes are not guaranteed. Do not treat an illustrative plantation investment return or project model as a fixed yield. Review assumptions and downside cases.

Process & Documentation

What steps should I take before investing?+

Begin by reading the relevant brochure and offer materials, then clarify the structure and questions specific to your circumstances. For the Harale opportunity, the briefing outlines an initial discussion, site visit, due diligence, investment agreement and capital deployment. Before committing funds, independently verify title records, survey details, access, approvals, costs, obligations, payment mechanics and exit provisions. Do not rely on a presentation alone.

What documents are mentioned for a debenture subscription?+

The Growth Fund presentation lists board-approved private debenture issuance, a debenture subscription agreement, allotment records, KYC documentation and conversion-option terms. Ask to review the applicable final versions and confirm that they match the offer being made to you. The legal effect depends on the actual executed documents and applicable law. Obtain independent legal, tax and investment advice before subscribing.

What land records should I review for the Harale opportunity?+

The project briefing specifically recommends reviewing cadastral mapping and RTC/title records as part of formal due diligence. It also says title, costs, approvals and other project facts require independent verification. Ask for the relevant title chain, survey boundaries, encumbrance information, access documentation, applicable permissions and signed agreements, as appropriate. The precise document set and legal requirements should be confirmed by an independent property lawyer.

Risk & Safety

What risks should I understand before making an investment?+

Both options carry risk. Debenture payments depend on the issuer’s obligations and ability to pay; land-linked outcomes depend on title, approvals, development costs, market demand, sales timing, taxes and execution. The Harale briefing explicitly notes that its return illustrations are not guaranteed and may change materially. Invest only after understanding the downside, liquidity limits, legal structure and whether you can afford to lose or lock up the capital.

Can I exit or get my money back whenever I need it?+

Do not assume either opportunity is immediately liquid or offers an on-demand refund. A debenture’s repayment, extension and conversion options are governed by its executed terms. A land-linked project’s exit and distribution mechanics depend on the final project agreements, sales and collections. Ask for the specific exit clauses, transfer restrictions, timing conditions and default remedies in writing before committing.

Should I get independent legal and tax advice?+

Yes. The Harale briefing says tax treatment and legal implications depend on each investor’s circumstances and applicable law, and advises obtaining qualified legal, tax and investment advice. The same care is sensible for a debenture subscription. An independent adviser can review the instrument, ownership structure, title records, regulatory position, tax consequences, risk disclosures and exit clauses without relying solely on the promoter’s explanation.
